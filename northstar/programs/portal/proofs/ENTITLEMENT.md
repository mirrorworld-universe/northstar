# Who authorized this SPL payout?

For the existing V2 checkpoint path, a settled token payout must correspond to **one occurrence** of a successful `StartWithdrawal` authorized by the ER holding's owner. It must pay the SPL token account named in that call, for the same mint, raw amount and decimals. The destination may belong to a third party. Two identical authorized calls entitle the destination to two payments, not one. One call cannot be split into two plan rows even if their sum is correct.

## Where the rule is enforced

1. [The bridge](../../token-bridge/src/processor.rs) checks the effective SVM signer against the owner of the loaded holding, the session bridge and the mint before debiting the holding. [The V2 recorder](../../../src/ephemeral_tx_client/continuity_v2.rs) uses the same authority check when it issues an intent. It processes successful ER transactions, resolves the registered bridge and the instruction's accounts, and records the chosen destination and amount. A top-level call needs a message signer; an inner call uses the signer's privileges in the successful invocation. It does not require a PDA to sign the outer transaction.
2. [Sealed-plan validation](../../../src/lineage_store_v2/token_entitlement.rs) reads withdrawal effects only from the sealed prefix. Mint comes from the touched holding's committed post-state, **not** from the plan being checked. Validation compares multisets of `(bridge program, session bridge, source holding, mint, destination token account, raw amount, decimals)`. It also checks the vault PDA and, in plan order per vault, that every positive amount advances to a distinct cumulative target. Only the first target lacks a sealed predecessor; the existing planner and on-chain counter check bind that step to the actual posted counter. First bind, reads and reopen all check the rule. A self-consistent checksum is not enough. Account, instruction, digest and capsule encodings are unchanged; an older inconsistent capsule fails closed on reopen.
3. L1 continues to check the registered vault and SPL accounts, the settled checkpoint and the [Portal ticket](token_authorization.rs). The ticket identifies the [full payout transcript](PAYOUTS.md). A new posting requires an exact counter advance and observed debit and credit at the vault and destination; a retry cannot consume the ticket again. No additional L1 signer is introduced.

## What Verus proves

The [composed proof](../../token-bridge/proofs/recipient_entitlement.rs) checks the [production entitlement routines](../../token-bridge/src/entitlement.rs), payout encoder, Portal ticket transition and bridge accounting/custody routines. For arbitrary finite lists, acceptance pairs every occurrence and preserves the number of copies of **any** intent. For any two positions along a valid per-vault target sequence, the later target is strictly greater. For an arbitrary matched row, successful composition implies owner authority, the exact requested fields in the actual payout, and exact observed movement. An explicit witness posts a payment to a different recipient and checks the success result. There are separate duplicate-request and replay checks.

**Trust boundary.** The proof starts with an authentic V2 execution record. It trusts SVM signature/CPI privileges and transaction atomicity, the registered bridge implementation, account/PDA decoding, completeness of the recorded calls and the sealed journal, and Rust sorting to permute rather than change the rows. It assumes SHA-256 binds the two compared authorization transcripts; injectivity of the pre-hash encoding is proved, not collision resistance of SHA-256. The bank, SPL Token, compiler and deployed binary are not verified here. L1 does not independently authenticate the original ER call against a validator presenting different history. This is payment safety, not a claim that payment eventually occurs.
